Also known as Akṣapāda (meaning "one who has eyes on his feet," signifying his absolute, unbroken absorption in philosophical contemplation), Maharṣi Gautama is the illustrious founder of the Nyāya Darśana.

He recognized that spiritual truth must withstand the most rigorous logical scrutiny to be universally valid. To prevent philosophical discourse from descending into dogma or sophistry, Gautama engineered a monumental, systematic framework for rational inquiry and the attainment of valid knowledge.

Nyāya Sūtras

Authored the foundational text establishing the definitive framework for Indian logic.

Four Pramāṇas

Formalized perception, inference, comparison, and reliable testimony as valid knowledge.

16 Padārthas

Enumerated 16 categories of investigation, including doubt, purpose, and fallacy.

Five-Part Syllogism

Created a robust, structured method for formulating and proving logical arguments.

Philosophical Scrutiny

Insisted that even spiritual claims must survive rigorous epistemological examination.

Vāda-Vidyā

Established formal rules of intellectual debate, protecting discourse from dogma.

Intellectual Arena

Established the ground rules for all ancient Indian philosophical debates (Śāstrārtha).

Mandatory Mastery

Nyāya logic became a strict prerequisite for scholars across all traditions.

Modern Relevance

His rules of epistemology continue to be studied globally in modern comparative logic.

Navya-Nyāya

Laid the groundwork for the hyper-formalized system of mathematical logic centuries later.

Pluralistic Realism

Posited that the external world is composed of eternal atoms and exists independently.
